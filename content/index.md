## whoami

I started my cybersecurity journey early, mainly by hacking into my dad's laptop to play video games. I wasn't sure what I wanted to do, so I went to college to study computer engineering. I worked in my college's IT department for a couple of years, played around with Windows and Linux administration, and experimented with networking, but I wasn't satisfied. Computers were fun, but I liked understanding how things worked at a deeper level. That's when I turned to embedded systems. I started developing ARM assembly code for class projects and personal experiments, diving into the low-level workings of hardware and firmware. I also liked RISC, because well, it was less instructions to memorize. I eventually became a computer engineer, but "specialized in embedded systems and IoT" during my undergraduate studies.

Then I was introduced to the SystemsCyber group, which demonstrated a [truck drive-by attack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SwtTzk9ys20). I loved the idea of real-world consequences, as it puts hacking into an entirely different perspective. No later than a year later, I went on to discover vulnerabilities and develop automotive security tooling of my own.

Why Bottoms Up? The phrase describes raising a glass and emptying its contents (typically into one's mouth). When working with systems, you quickly become familiar with top-down and bottom-up engineering (my thesis was based on this). I combined the two ideas: as much as I like beer, I love reverse engineering a system until it becomes open source. Hence the motto, “Everything is open source if you know where to look.” Take a look at my [[blog/index|Bottoms Up]] blog for more.
